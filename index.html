Pulsar Emission-Geometry Parameters

Polarization observations provide one of the most direct probes of pulsar magnetospheric geometry and emission physics. In the Rotating Vector Model, the position-angle swing is governed by the magnetic inclination angle α and the impact angle β as the emission cone sweeps across the observer.

Scientific Context

The Rotating Vector Model (RVM) provides a geometrical framework for interpreting pulsar polarization. In this model, the observed swing of the linear polarization position angle across pulse phase traces the projected dipolar magnetic field and constrains the viewing geometry between the pulsar magnetic axis and the observer's line of sight.

With the rapid development of large radio telescopes such as FAST and MeerKAT, together with high-sensitivity surveys including GPPS and the MeerKAT Thousand Pulsar Array, the number of pulsars with polarization measurements has increased substantially. This enables systematic, survey-scale RVM fitting and the construction of homogeneous pulsar geometry catalogues.
